Read the pawn push. What does White want next?

After a3, White can support b4. From b4 the pawn attacks c5, and moving Black’s c-pawn away would make d4 easier for White. In the reference, try 3.d4. White has already played the central break, so Black can take on d4 immediately.

Trace the bishop’s diagonal. What stands in its way?

After b4 and ...Bg7, trace g7–f6–e5–d4–c3–b2–a1. At first the bishop attacks the rook. Nc3 blocks the diagonal and leaves the knight tied to a1. In the reference, try an early Rb1 and trace it again. Moving the rook changes what Black can win.

Choose a central square. Can Black develop with a threat?

In the teaching line, ...d6 supports c5. When b5 attacks the knight, d4 is available. Compare 4.c3 in the reference. A white pawn occupies the knight’s usual c3-square, and Black can challenge e4 straight away with ...d5.
