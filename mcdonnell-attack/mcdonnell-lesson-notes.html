01

The starting decision

Challenge the centre first

Video 3:06
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5

After 1.e4 c5 2.f4, the video's repertoire plays 2...d5. Black immediately asks White to make a decision about the e4-pawn.

White's f-pawn has left f2. Keep an eye on the diagonal leading towards g1, especially after White castles. An attack still needs open lines and developed pieces.

  • 3.e5 closes the centre. Develop and find useful squares behind the pawns.
  • 3.exd5 opens the centre. Use ...Nf6 and development before collecting the pawn.
  • 3.Nf3, 3.Nc3, 3.Bb5+ and 3.d3 each require their own response.
RememberOur first move is 2...d5. The next move depends on White's reply.
Ask MisikaWhat does 2...d5 ask White to decide?
Whether to push the e4-pawn, capture on d5, defend it, or allow Black to capture it. Say which choice White has made before selecting Black's next move.
02

3.e5 and 5.Be2

Develop towards f5

Video 3:28
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.e5 Nc6 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.Be2 e6 6.O-O Nge7 7.d3 Nf5

With White's pawn on e5, f6 is attacked. Black develops the b8-knight to c6, puts the bishop on g4 and uses ...e6 to free the f8-bishop.

The g8-knight can reach f5 through e7 or h6. In the displayed line, its route is g8-e7-f5. The move is written Nge7 because the c6-knight could also move to e7.

Before Be2, ...Bg4 pins the f3-knight to the queen on d1. After Be2, the bishop on e2 interrupts that line, so the pin is no longer the same.

RememberChoose a destination for the knight. Here f5 puts pressure on d4 and brings the knight close to the king.
Ask MisikaWhy does Black use e7 or h6 instead of f6?
White's e5-pawn attacks f6. The route through e7 or h6 avoids that pawn and aims for f5.
Ask MisikaWhich pawn break is White trying to prepare with c3?
White wants d4. Count Black's attackers on d4 before deciding whether that break works.
03

Two different attacking positions

The ...h5 idea needs care

Video 4:30
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.e5 Nc6 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.Be2 e6 6.O-O Nge7 7.d3 Nf5 8.c3 h5 9.h3 Bxf3 10.Bxf3 Be7

With 8.Ne1 instead of 8.c3, the video shows 8...h5 9.Bxg4 hxg4 10.Qxg4 c4. If White accepts with 11.dxc4, ...Bc5+ opens the attack along the diagonal to g1. After Kh1, ...Rh4 gains time against the queen.

The diagram comes from the separate 8.c3 line. After ...h5 and h3, Black exchanges with ...Bxf3 rather than leaving the bishop to be taken. Then ...Be7 completes the minor-piece development.

In this position 11.Bxh5 permits 11...Ng3. The knight attacks the rook on f1 and the bishop on h5. Against 11.Na3, the video continues with ...Bh4.

RememberA pawn sacrifice is a calculation, not a rule. Check the exact piece placement before copying ...h5.
Ask MisikaAfter Bxh5 Ng3, which two pieces are attacked?
The rook on f1 and the bishop on h5. That is why the h5-pawn is not simply free.
Ask MisikaWhat does ...c4 achieve in the 8.Ne1 line?
If White answers dxc4, the d3-pawn leaves the a7-g1 diagonal. Black can develop with ...Bc5+ and bring more pieces into the attack.
04

3.e5 and 7.c3

Open the diagonal with ...c4

Video 6:17
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.e5 Nc6 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.Be2 e6 6.O-O Nge7 7.c3 Nf5 8.Ne1 Bxe2 9.Qxe2 c4 10.d4 cxd3 11.Nxd3 Qb6+ 12.Kh1 h5

After 7.c3 Nf5 8.Ne1, Black first exchanges the e2-bishop with ...Bxe2. After Qxe2, ...c4 makes White decide what to do with the d-pawn.

The sequence d2-d4 c4xd3 is en passant. Black's pawn lands on d3, and the white pawn disappears from d4. The capture must happen immediately after White's two-square pawn move.

After Nxd3, the knight controls c5, so Black uses ...Qb6+ instead of placing the bishop on c5. The follow-up ...h5 prepares ...h4 and possible ...Ng3+ ideas.

RememberEn passant changes the pawn structure immediately. Name the landing square correctly: cxd3.
Ask MisikaAfter ...c4 and d2-d4, where does Black's pawn land if it captures en passant?
On d3. White's pawn on d4 is removed. The move is ...cxd3, not ...cxd4.
Ask MisikaWhy is ...Qb6+ used after Nxd3?
The knight on d3 attacks c5, so it can capture a bishop placed there. The queen checks along b6-c5-d4-e3-f2-g1.
05

3.e5 and 5.Bb5

Keep the structure in view

Video 6:57
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.e5 Nc6 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.Bb5 Qb6 6.Bxc6+ Qxc6 7.O-O e6 8.d3 Ne7 9.h3 Bxf3 10.Qxf3 Nf5

After 5.Bb5, ...Qb6 attacks the bishop. If White plays Bxc6+, recapture with the queen. The same ...e6 and knight-to-f5 plan remains useful.

In the 8.d3 line, the video meets h3 with ...Bxf3. After Qxf3 Nf5, compare the central pawns and the remaining minor pieces. Queens are still on the board, so keep checking king safety.

The 8.c3 branch differs. After ...h5 9.d4 cxd4 10.cxd4 Nh6, the d4-pawn is a target for ...Nf5. The alternative 9.h3 Nh6 10.hxg4 hxg4 11.Ng5 c4 is a separate bishop-sacrifice line. It needs its own calculation.

RememberA small move-order change can alter whether Black exchanges a bishop or sacrifices it.
Ask MisikaWhy does Black recapture Bxc6+ with the queen?
The queen on b6 can take the bishop on c6, answer the check, and keep Black's pawn structure intact.
Ask MisikaAfter ...cxd4 cxd4, which white pawn becomes a clear target?
The pawn on d4. The knight can go to f5 to attack it, and the bishop on g4 can pressure its defender on f3.
Ask MisikaIn the c3 branch, does Nxd4 Bxd1 simply win a queen?
Continue calculating. White has Nxc6, taking Black's queen in return. The video then shows ...Be2 Rf2 bxc6 Rxe2. Both queens are traded, so count all the captures before judging the result.
06

3.e5, 5.Bb5 and 6.Nc3

Answer threats before taking material

Video 9:32
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.e5 Nc6 4.Nf3 Bg4 5.Bb5 Qb6 6.Nc3 d4 7.Nd5 Qd8

After 6.Nc3, Black can push ...d4 with an attack on the knight. White can exchange on c6 and retreat the knight, or try 7.Nd5.

Against Nd5, the video chooses ...Qd8. Taking the bishop with ...Qxb5 would allow Nc7+, forking the king and queen. Once the immediate threat is handled, ...e6 is a useful way to challenge the knight on d5.

In the exchange branch, Black can trade queens and use ...d3, cxd3 and ...Rd8 to put pressure on White's doubled d-pawns. Follow the exact line on the board before judging the resulting position.

RememberBefore capturing a loose piece, check the opponent's forcing reply.
Ask MisikaWhy is ...Qxb5 dangerous after Nd5?
Nc7+ would attack the king on e8 and the queen on b5 at the same time. Black must answer the check, so the queen is lost.
07

3.exd5

Use the tempo to develop

Video 10:18
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.exd5 Nf6 4.c4 e6 5.dxe6 Bxe6 6.Nf3 Nc6 7.Nc3 g6 8.Be2 Bg7 9.O-O O-O

After 3.exd5, the video chooses ...Nf6. Black delays recapturing the pawn and brings a piece out. If White supports the pawn with c4, ...e6 challenges it again.

The diagram shows 4.c4 e6 5.dxe6 Bxe6. Black is a pawn down, but the bishops and knights are active. That activity is the reason for the pawn investment; it does not make a win automatic.

If White develops with Nc3 or Nf3 instead, ...Nxd5 usually recovers the pawn. A Bb5+ check is met with ...Bd7. The right recapture on d7 depends on the move order: sometimes the queen must defend the knight on d5.

RememberWhen deciding how to recapture, ask what that piece must defend next.
Ask MisikaWhat has Black received for the pawn in the diagram?
Active bishops, both knights developed, and a castled king. Look for targets and useful files instead of assuming that compensation guarantees a win.
Ask MisikaWhy should we avoid memorising 'always Nxd7' after Bxd7+?
In the line with 4.Nc3 Nxd5 5.Bb5+ Bd7 6.Bxd7+, Black uses ...Qxd7 to keep the d5-knight defended. The exact move order matters.
08

3.Nf3

Take the pawn, then stay active

Video 13:59
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.Nf3 dxe4 4.Ng5 Bf5 5.Bc4 e6 6.Nc3 Nf6 7.Qe2 Nc6 8.Ngxe4 Nd4

After 3.Nf3, Black captures with ...dxe4. Against Ng5, ...Bf5 protects the pawn. If White adds Bc4, meet the threat to f7 with ...e6.

The video's line develops with ...Nf6 and ...Nc6 even when White adds another attacker to e4. After Ngxe4, ...Nd4 attacks the queen on e2 and threatens the c2 square. Development can matter more than holding the extra pawn forever.

Against 4.Ne5, Black instead uses ...g6. In the Bc4 branch, ...Nh6 protects f7 and ...f6 later challenges the knight. Against an immediate Bb5+, ...Nd7 answers the check, followed by ...Nf6 in the Nc3 line.

If White tries 5.g4 against ...Bf5, the video retreats to ...Bd7. After Nxe4, ...Nf6 offers a knight trade while White has already moved the kingside pawns.

RememberDo not spend every move defending one extra pawn. Compare its value with development and threats.
Ask MisikaWhy does ...e6 matter when White has Ng5 and Bc4?
Those pieces attack f7 together. The pawn on e6 blocks the bishop's diagonal from c4 to f7.
Ask MisikaAfter Ngxe4, what does ...Nd4 attack immediately?
The queen on e2. It also puts c2 under attack. The opponent has to respond while Black improves a piece.
09

3.Nc3 and 4.Nce2

Use ...d4 to gain space and time

Video 16:23
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.Nc3 d4 4.Nce2 Qa5 5.Nf3 Nf6 6.e5 Nd5 7.g3 Bf5 8.Bg2 Ne3

After 3.Nc3, ...d4 gains space and attacks the knight. Following Nce2, the video uses ...Qa5 and ...Nf6. If White pushes e5, the knight goes to d5.

The sequence g3 Bf5 Bg2 Ne3 attacks a queen with very few squares. White has a tactical resource: b4 attacks Black's queen. The video continues ...Qxb4 c3 Nxg2, so do not stop calculating at the first queen attack.

White's stronger alternative in the video is Ng3 instead of e5. Black uses ...h5 to threaten ...h4. After h4, ...Nc6 brings out another piece. In the separate Bb5+ branch, exchanges on d7 lead to a ...d3 pawn sacrifice that brings the queen to d3.

RememberA trapped-looking queen is a prompt to calculate. Check counterattacks against your own queen too.
Ask MisikaAfter ...Ne3, which move can White use to attack Black's queen?
b4 attacks the queen on a5. In the video, ...Qxb4 is followed by c3, attacking the queen again. Black then takes the g2-bishop. Play through the whole sequence before counting the gain.
Ask MisikaWhat does ...h5 threaten against a knight on g3?
...h4 would attack the knight. The video answers White's h4 with ...Nc6, continuing development.
10

3.Bb5+

Exchange, develop, choose a king square

Video 18:18
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.Bb5+ Bd7 4.Bxd7+ Qxd7 5.d3 Nc6 6.Nf3 O-O-O

Against the immediate 3.Bb5+, block with ...Bd7. After Bxd7+ Qxd7, Black has a clear route to queenside castling once the b8-knight develops.

The diagram shows 5.d3 Nc6 6.Nf3 O-O-O. Black activates the rook on the d-file. The video also gives ...e6 instead of castling immediately, allowing a quieter development plan.

After the exchanges, 5.e5 is met with ...Nc6. The exd5 move order connects to the capture lines. Against 5.Nc3, the video uses ...dxe4, Nxe4 Nf6, Nxf6+ exf6, then ...Nc6 after Nf3.

RememberCastling is also a rook move. Look at the file the rook will occupy.
Ask MisikaWhat does castling queenside do besides moving the king?
It puts the a8-rook on d8 in one move. Here that rook supports Black's central play down the d-file.
11

3.d3

Use the open file after a queen trade

Video 20:26
1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5 3.d3 Nc6 4.Nf3 dxe4 5.dxe4 Qxd1+ 6.Kxd1 Nf6 7.Nc3 Bg4 8.Be2 O-O-O+ 9.Bd2

After 3.d3 Nc6 4.Nf3, the video simplifies with ...dxe4 dxe4 Qxd1+ Kxd1. White's king loses the right to castle, and the d-file opens.

Black develops with ...Nf6 and ...Bg4. After Be2, ...O-O-O+ brings the rook to d8 with check. White answers Bd2. Even with queens exchanged, active pieces and king placement still matter.

If White plays 4.e5 instead of Nf3, Black uses ...Nh6 with the familiar aim of reaching f5. Recognise the plan, then check the new position before following a remembered line.

RememberWhen queens come off, find the open file and improve your least active piece.
Ask MisikaWhy does ...O-O-O give check in this line?
The rook lands on d8 and looks down the open d-file at the white king on d1. Bd2 then blocks the line.
Ask MisikaWhat changes permanently after Kxd1?
White's king has moved, so White can no longer castle. Black can use the open d-file to gain time against that king.

Five minutes of practice

Extra class exercise based on the positions above.

  1. Set up 1.e4 c5 2.f4 d5. Let the coach choose e5, exd5, Nf3, Nc3, Bb5+ or d3. Name Black's response and its purpose.

  3. Return to the position before the move you found hardest. Find one sensible alternative for White and analyse it with the coach.
